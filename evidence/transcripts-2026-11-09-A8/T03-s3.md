# Executive Workshop Proposal

**Client:** Tamarack Instruments

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff.

The Client's AI use spans three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

AI maturity is at the piloting stage. An internal audit finding from June 2026 records that there is no inventory of AI systems.

**Regulatory and certification context:** The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is expected to start next year, and the start date is not fixed.

**Incumbent frameworks:** The Client operates an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function.

**Stated constraints:**

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

**Stated objective:** Dana Whitfield's objective is: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

**Timeline:** A decision is expected by 15 December 2026.

## 2. Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

At the piloting stage, the governance choices with the most leverage are made before systems scale. These choices are:

- who owns AI governance
- how much AI risk the organization accepts
- how oversight runs
- what budget governance carries

These are executive decisions. A single structured day brings the people who hold them into one room to make them together, rather than across a sequence of separate meetings.

The Q2 2027 objective gives the session a fixed horizon to plan against. A decision is expected by 15 December 2026. Whether the Workshop should be scheduled before that date is a question to confirm at intake.

## 3. Four-Block Structure with Client-Specific Emphasis

The Workshop runs as four 90-minute blocks with breaks. The program draws on two frameworks:

- the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

Two further resources serve as references:

- AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) at the board layer
- The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done) at the acceptance layer

**Block 1: Alignment.** The room reviews the three use cases by stage: deployed, piloting and planned. The June 2026 finding on the absent AI inventory serves as the shared starting point. The block produces a shared statement of governance intent.

**Block 2: Principles.** The room tests governance operating principles against the Client's context, including the three Academy principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

- The visual inspection pilot gives the discussion a concrete case. Where human judgment should sit on inspection outcomes is a question for the room to decide.
- The works council consultation requirement and the EU data residency requirement are treated as boundary conditions on any principle adopted.

The block produces agreed principles ranked by the room.

**Block 3: Roadmap.** The room sequences governance work against the EU AI Act, GDPR and the ISO/IEC 42001 target.

The block produces a draft 12-month roadmap with owners, set against the Q2 2027 production objective.

**Block 4: Decisions.** The room works through an executive decision session covering four areas:

- governance ownership

- budget mandate

The block closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix* ([facilitation kit](https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-Governance-Academy/blob/main/the-turtle-and-the-phoenix.md)) and the kit's twenty-minute closing exercise. This serves as the retention device for the three principles.
