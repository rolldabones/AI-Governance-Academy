# Guided AI GRC Build: Proposal for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Guided AI GRC Build, twelve weeks, US$60,000

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It has about 1,100 staff, is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States. The EU AI Act and GDPR are its stated regulatory drivers.

The Client's AI use spans three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. The Client operates an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function. It intends to pursue ISO/IEC 42001, with work to start next year and the start date not yet fixed.

The stated objective, in Dana Whitfield's words, is: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.

- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

## 2. Why a Guided Build Rather Than Consulting Delivery

A consulting engagement would hand Tamarack Instruments a finished set of documents. A guided build hands over a working system and a team that knows how to run it.

In the Guided AI GRC Build, the Client's own build team produces every artifact during structured asynchronous work between sessions. A weekly workshop teaches the method and a weekly coaching session tests the work. At Week 12, the people who will operate the AI Management System (AIMS) are the people who built it. They understand why each control exists and can maintain it without outside dependency.

This matters for Tamarack Instruments for three reasons:

1. **It works within systems the Client already runs.** The build attaches to the existing ISMS and quality management system rather than standing up a parallel structure.
2. **It builds operating evidence.** By Week 12 the system has been running and producing records, which is what later ISO/IEC 42001 readiness work will need to show.
3. **It applies three principles throughout:** Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

The program draws on two open frameworks:

- GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) provides the governance artifacts.
- Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) provides the governed workflow method.

AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) serves as the reference for board-layer reporting at the steering checkpoints.
