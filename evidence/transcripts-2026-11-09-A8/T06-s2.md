# Guided AI GRC Build

**Proposal for Tamarack Instruments**
**Prepared for Dana Whitfield, General Counsel**

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage. Its three AI use cases are at different stages:

* **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
* **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
* **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 is a certification target. Work toward it is to start next year, and the start date is not fixed. In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded no inventory of AI systems.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

Three constraints shape the build:

* Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
* Customer data must stay in the EU.
* The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

The program fee of US$60,000 sits within the approved budget figure. Whether the program falls within the current fiscal year is a question to confirm at intake.

## 2. The Case for a Guided Build

A conventional consulting engagement delivers a governance system that the Client receives but did not build. The Guided AI GRC Build reverses this. Over twelve weeks the Client's own build team constructs each artifact between sessions, using open frameworks and supported by one live virtual workshop and one coaching session each week. The team will be drawn from these roles:

* General Counsel
* CISO
* Head of Quality
* Head of Data
* German plant manager
* Two internal auditors

At Week 12 the people who will run the Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) are the people who designed it. They understand why each control exists, can explain it to an auditor and can adapt it when visual inspection and sales forecasting change the risk picture. ISO/IEC 42001 work next year would then start from an operating system rather than a document set.

The program draws on two open frameworks:

* GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) for the governance, risk and compliance structure.
* Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) for governed workflows and human oversight.

Three principles run throughout: Slow AI, Informed Intent and Final Liability rests with the Human.

Including two internal auditors in the build team strengthens evidence design. It also raises an independence question. We propose that the internal auditors shape evidence requirements and lead the Week 12 readiness review, without owning operating controls. How the internal audit function prefers to preserve its independence is a question to confirm at intake.
