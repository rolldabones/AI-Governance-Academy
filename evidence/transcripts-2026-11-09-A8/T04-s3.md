# Proposal: Practitioner Academy for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Practitioner Academy, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** 2 days, live virtual, four 90-minute working sessions per day
**Price:** US$12,500

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States, and has about 1,100 staff. The Client's AI maturity is at the piloting stage. It has three AI use cases:

- A customer service chatbot deployed on the support portal.
- An AI visual inspection pilot on the German production line.
- AI sales forecasting planned for next year.

The Client's regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, with the start date not yet fixed. In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. The Client operates an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS and an ISO 9001 quality management system and has an internal audit function.

The objective stated by Dana Whitfield is: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

The program works within three stated constraints:

- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.
- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.

## 2. Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The Client has an AI system in deployment, another in pilot and a third planned. It also has a dated audit finding on inventory and a stated production milestone for visual inspection. Together these give the program concrete working material and a fixed point to build toward.

The Practitioner Academy has participants start the instruments themselves rather than receive them finished:

- an inventory
- risk register entries
- governed workflows
- an evidence plan

Participants work directly in two repositories:

- the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

They leave knowing where each artifact lives and how to continue it unaided. Three principles carry through every session: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

The program does not promise any certification or audit outcome. It builds the working capability that ISO/IEC 42001 preparation and audit follow-up both depend on.

## 3. Running Worked Examples

Three use cases run across both days, each chosen for a specific reason:

- **Customer service chatbot (deployed).** It is the Client's live system. It also exercises the EU data residency constraint in prompt and workflow design.
- **AI visual inspection (pilot).** It is the subject of the stated Q2 2027 objective. It also exercises human oversight and the works council constraint.
- **AI sales forecasting (planned).** It tests the intake path a new system follows into the inventory before it is built.

All exercises use sanitized analogs: synthetic support queries, illustrative inspection records and mock forecast inputs. No customer data or production staff data enters the program. Whether any exercise touching visual inspection requires works council consultation is a question to confirm at intake.
