# Executive Workshop Proposal

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel, Tamarack Instruments
**Program:** Executive Workshop, AI Governance Academy
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It has about 1,100 staff and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States, with headquarters in the Netherlands. The Client's AI activity is at three stages. A customer service chatbot is deployed on the support portal. AI visual inspection is being piloted on the German production line. AI sales forecasting is planned for next year.

The stated objective is clear: governance must be in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027. An internal audit finding from June 2026 recorded that no inventory of AI systems exists. That finding is the most concrete governance gap in the record, and it bears directly on the objective. Governance cannot be shown to cover systems that have not been identified.

The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system and an ISO 9001 quality management system. ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected to start next year, although the start date is not fixed. The EU AI Act and GDPR are the named regulatory drivers. Three constraints shape the work. Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year. Customer data must stay in the EU. The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

## Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

The Client's AI maturity is recorded as Piloting. At this stage the decisions that matter most are structural: who owns AI governance, how much AI risk the organization will accept, how often leadership reviews it and what budget supports it. These decisions need executive alignment before practitioner effort is committed. A single day is enough to settle them without delaying the work that follows.

Timing also favors a short format. The Profile Block records a decision expected by 15 December 2026, although the subject of that decision is `Unknown`. A workshop held before that date gives leadership a documented basis for it. Working back from Q2 2027 leaves limited time for the inventory, risk assessment and controls that visual inspection will require. An agreed roadmap in December makes better use of that window than one assembled later in the first quarter.

The workshop fee of US$7,500 sits within the approved fiscal-year budget of US$80,000. Most of that budget remains available for whatever implementation work the roadmap identifies.

## Structure and Client-Specific Emphasis

The workshop runs as four 90-minute blocks with breaks. It draws on two frameworks: the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen). It also uses AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) as the board-layer reference and The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done) as the acceptance-layer reference.

**Block 1, Alignment,** reviews the Client's current AI posture across the deployed chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned forecasting use case. It treats the June 2026 audit finding as the starting point. The room produces a shared statement of governance intent. Where the record does not show how a system currently operates or who holds responsibility for it, the facilitator will record that as `Unknown` and turn it into a question for the room.

**Block 2, Principles,** sets governance operating principles for the Client's context. It includes the three Academy principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent and Final Liability rests with the Human. Discussion will cover two issues the record raises:

- **Visual inspection and production work:** how the principles apply where AI may affect production work. Whether the pilot touches production staff, and so engages the works council consultation constraint, is `Unknown` and will be raised as a question.
- **The customer-facing chatbot:** how the principles apply to customer interactions. Whether the chatbot processes customer data, and where that data resides, is `Unknown` and will be raised against the EU data residency constraint.

The room ranks the agreed principles.

**Block 3, Roadmap,** sequences governance work against the EU AI Act, GDPR, the ISO/IEC 42001 target and the incumbent frameworks. AI governance is positioned as an extension of the existing ISMS and quality management system rather than a parallel structure. ISO/IEC 42001 follows the same harmonized management system structure as ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001, which supports this approach. The record does not show which registers, processes, controls or records exist within those systems. Specific attachment points, such as a risk register, an asset inventory or a change control process, are therefore `Unknown` and will be confirmed in the session. The block produces a draft 12-month roadmap with owners, sequenced backward from the Q2 2027 objective.

**Block 4, Decisions,** produces a decision log covering four areas: governance ownership, risk appetite for AI, oversight cadence and budget mandate. The block closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix* and the twenty-minute closing exercise from the facilitation kit (https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-Governance-Academy/blob/main/the-turtle-and-the-phoenix.md). This closing exercise is the retention device for the three principles.

## Regulatory Orientation

Regulatory content in the workshop is orientation, not legal advice. The following questions should be confirmed with external counsel before the roadmap is finalized:

- how the EU AI Act classifies the visual inspection system and the customer service chatbot
- what transparency obligations apply to the chatbot
- which GDPR requirements apply to any personal data the systems process

The workshop makes no promise of ISO/IEC 42001 certification or of any audit outcome. It prepares leadership to direct that work.
