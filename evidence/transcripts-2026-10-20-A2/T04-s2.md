## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in Germany and the United States. Client's AI portfolio sits at three stages:

* **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
* **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
* **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that Client holds no inventory of AI systems. The regulatory drivers named are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is expected to begin next year, but the start date is not fixed.

Your stated objective is clear: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." Three constraints shape this proposal:

* Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
* Customer data must stay in the EU.
* The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

## 2. Why Practitioner Capability Fits Now

**The inventory gap is practical work.** An AI inventory is built by people who know where AI is used and how. The Practitioner Academy puts your practitioners inside the GRC Workbook to start that inventory with their own systems. It does not hand them a template to fill in later.

**The pilot window is open now.** Visual inspection is still in pilot. Classification under the EU AI Act, risk and impact assessment and the design of human oversight are easier to shape before production than after. Waiting until Q2 2027 leaves little room to act on what those assessments find.

**The named roles span the governance surface.** The audience roles are the General Counsel, the CISO, the Head of Quality, the Head of Data, the German plant manager and two internal auditors. Together they cover legal, security, quality, data, operations and assurance. Working the same examples together gives them a shared method and shared artifacts.

**Incumbent frameworks are a reference point, not a starting assumption.** Client has named ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 as incumbent frameworks. Whether any existing Client records or procedures can be reused for AI governance is `Unknown`. Participants will assess that during the program rather than assume it.

The program is anchored in three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

## 3. Worked Examples

All three of Client's use cases run as worked examples across both days, because each represents a different governance situation:

* **Customer service chatbot (deployed).** This tests governance applied to a live system. It brings the EU data residency constraint and GDPR into every exercise.
* **AI visual inspection (piloting).** This is the system tied directly to your Q2 2027 objective. It brings EU AI Act classification, quality acceptance decisions and the works council constraint into scope.
* **AI sales forecasting (planned).** This shows how a system enters governance before it is built. It gives the inventory and classification exercises a pre-deployment entry.

**Sanitized analogs.** All exercises use sanitized analogs in place of real data:

* Synthetic support transcripts replace real chatbot conversations, so no customer data is used.
* Described inspection scenarios with no imagery of personnel replace production data.
* Fictional figures replace real sales data.

**Works council constraint.** The program itself does not touch production staff. Any workflow drafted for visual inspection that would affect production staff will carry an explicit works council consultation gate. That workflow is not for use until consultation has taken place. The current status of any works council consultation is `Unknown`.
