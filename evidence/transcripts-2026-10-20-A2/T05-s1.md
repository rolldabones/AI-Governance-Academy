# Proposal: Implementation Accelerator for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel (Sponsor)
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026
**Program:** Implementation Accelerator, AI Governance Academy

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It has about 1,100 staff and is headquartered in the Netherlands, with operations in Germany and the United States. The Client's AI portfolio is at three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

Three facts shape the governance need. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. The Sponsor's stated objective is to have governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027. ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected to start next year, with no fixed start date. The regulatory drivers named are the EU AI Act and GDPR.

The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system and an ISO 9001 quality management system. Those frameworks are the natural home for AI governance. This proposal does not assume any specific register, process, control or record within them. Those attachment points will be confirmed with the Client during the week.

Three stated constraints apply:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

## 2. Why an Intensive Foundation Build Fits Now

The Client has a decision expected by 15 December 2026 and a production milestone in Q2 2027. Between those points, the visual inspection pilot needs a defined route to production. That route requires a governance body with clear decision rights, a classified inventory, an assessed risk position, adopted policies and a way to evidence all of it. A five-day build establishes that foundation in one concentrated effort rather than spreading it across months of intermittent meetings. Implementation time remains before the Q2 2027 milestone.

The Accelerator also answers the June 2026 audit finding directly. The AI system inventory is a Day 2 deliverable, seeded with the three known use cases.

For ISO/IEC 42001, the artifacts are structured to support future conformity work when the Client begins it next year. This is readiness work. The Accelerator does not promise certification or any audit outcome.

At US$25,000, the Accelerator sits within the approved budget of up to US$80,000. Whether the session dates fall within the current fiscal year is Unknown, because the fiscal year boundaries are not stated.

The week is run on three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human. In practice, this means each AI system is examined deliberately before it scales, each decision to deploy is made with its purpose and limits understood, and accountability for outcomes is assigned to named people rather than to systems.

## 3. The Five-Day Build Spine, Tailored to Tamarack Instruments

The sequence follows two dependencies. The charter comes before policies, so policies have a body with authority to adopt them. The inventory comes before the risk register, so risks attach to known systems.

**Day 1: Governance charter and roles.**
The team drafts the AI governance charter covering:
- purpose and scope across the Netherlands, Germany and the United States
- roles and decision rights
- meeting cadence

The charter will define how the AI governance body relates to the existing ISMS and QMS governance. The specific interface points are Unknown and will be confirmed in session. The day also sets the decision path for moving an AI system from pilot to production, with visual inspection as the first case. Executive checkpoint attendance is required.

**Day 2: AI system inventory and classification.**
The team builds the inventory schema and enters the chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned sales forecasting system as the first entries. Classification fields will include:
- lifecycle stage
- data categories
- data location
- affected parties
- orientation-level EU AI Act risk category

EU AI Act classification is orientation only: confirm with external counsel. Any additional AI systems in use are Unknown and will be surfaced during the session.

**Day 3: Risk and impact assessment plus risk register.**
The team assesses each inventoried system and populates the risk register. All seeded risk statements are drafts for Client validation. The assessment will address several topics, each tested against the inventory rather than assumed:
- GDPR considerations
- the EU data residency constraint
- quality implications of AI visual inspection
- impacts on production staff

The register will be designed to attach to the ISMS. The specific attachment point is Unknown.

**Day 4: Core policies and operating procedures.**
The team drafts policies covering:
- acceptable use
- AI lifecycle governance
- vendor and supplier governance
- incident response
- change management

The procedures will include a step that confirms works council consultation before any AI system touches production staff. The legal requirements for that consultation are to be confirmed with external counsel. Interfaces with existing ISMS and QMS procedures are Unknown and will be mapped in session.
