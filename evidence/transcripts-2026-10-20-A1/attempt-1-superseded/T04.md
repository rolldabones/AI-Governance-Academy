# Practitioner Academy Proposal

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel, Tamarack Instruments
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026
**Program:** Practitioner Academy, AI Governance Academy

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments from its headquarters in the Netherlands, with operations in Germany and the United States. The Client has one AI system deployed (a customer service chatbot on the support portal), one in pilot (AI visual inspection on the German production line) and one planned for next year (AI sales forecasting).

Three pressures meet in the coming months:

- **Audit finding.** An internal audit finding from June 2026 recorded that no inventory of AI systems exists.
- **Production deadline.** The stated objective is to have governance in place before visual inspection enters production in Q2 2027.
- **Regulation and certification.** The EU AI Act and GDPR are the named regulatory drivers. ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected to begin next year, although its start date is not fixed.

The Client names ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 as incumbent frameworks. This proposal makes no assumption about which registers, procedures or controls exist under them. That is Unknown until confirmed in pre-work.

## Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The audit finding and the Q2 2027 deadline both require working artifacts rather than awareness. The Practitioner Academy has the people who will own those artifacts build them during the program, using their own systems as the material.

Your nominated cohort covers the accountabilities the visual inspection rollout will test:

- General Counsel and CISO
- Head of Quality and Head of Data
- The German plant manager
- Two internal auditors

Bringing them into one room for two days produces a shared method and a started body of work. The goal is not a set of parallel efforts that need reconciling later.

The program rests on three principles:

- Slow AI
- Informed Intent
- Final Liability rests with the Human

On Day 1 these are seated through the teaching fable *The Turtle and the Phoenix*. The facilitation kit's handle map then connects the story to the GRC Workbook modules before the instruments arrive on Day 2.

## Running Worked Examples

The two days are built around three of the Client's own use cases:

1. **AI visual inspection (primary).** This system drives the deadline. It touches Quality, Information Security and Data, and it raises works council and employee data questions at the German plant. Its EU AI Act classification is not prejudged here. Determining it is an exercise output.
2. **Customer service chatbot (secondary).** As the only deployed system, it shows how governance is applied retrospectively. It carries the EU data residency constraint and allows participants to test how transparency obligations under the EU AI Act may apply.
3. **AI sales forecasting (intake case).** This system is used on Day 1 only. It shows how a planned system enters the inventory and is classified before procurement or build begins.

## Curriculum Spine

Each day runs as four 90-minute working sessions.

| Session | Focus | Worked example | Repository asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1, S1 | Governance structures; *The Turtle and the Phoenix* told from the facilitation kit, closing with the kit's twenty-minute exercise | Visual inspection decision rights across Legal, Quality and plant | GRC Workbook, governance structures module; fable facilitation kit and handle map |
| Day 1, S2 | AI system inventories and classification | All three use cases entered and classified | GRC Workbook, inventory and classification module |
| Day 1, S3 | Risk and impact assessments | Visual inspection and chatbot | GRC Workbook, risk and impact assessment module |
| Day 1, S4 | Policies and operating procedures | Procedure outline for visual inspection go-live | GRC Workbook, policies and procedures module |

| Day 2, S8 | Evidence collection and audit readiness | Evidence set for the audit finding and for visual inspection go-live | GRC Workbook evidence material; The Definition of Done for acceptance criteria |

Participants work directly in the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) repositories. They leave knowing where each artifact lives and how to continue it.

## How the Program Respects Your Constraints

- **Customer data stays in the EU.** No customer data enters the sessions or the repositories. Chatbot exercises use sanitized analog transcripts written by the facilitator.
- **Works council consultation.** No exercise touches production staff, their data or images of them. The visual inspection workflow is designed on paper as a draft for future consultation and is not implemented. The works council consultation step is written into the workflow as a required gate.

## Deliverables

At the close of Day 2 the Client holds the following work in progress, owned by named participants:

- **Started AI system inventory.** It covers the chatbot, visual inspection and sales forecasting, with draft classifications.
- **Draft risk register entries.** These cover visual inspection and the chatbot.
- **At least two governed workflows.** These are inspection defect review and chatbot escalation, each with defined human oversight points.
- **Procedure outline** for visual inspection go-live.
- **Evidence checklist** aligned to the audit finding and to the Q2 2027 deadline.

These are started artifacts, not finished ones. The inventory responds directly to the June 2026 finding, but whether the finding is closed is a decision for internal audit. The artifacts are structured so they can carry forward into ISO/IEC 42001 work once it begins. The program does not promise any certification or audit outcome.
