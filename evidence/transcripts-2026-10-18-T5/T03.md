# Executive Workshop Proposal: Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments from its headquarters in the Netherlands, with operations in Germany and the United States. AI is present in the business at three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting for next year.

In June 2026 an internal audit found that the Client holds no inventory of AI systems. Your stated objective is clear: governance must be in place before visual inspection enters production in Q2 2027.

Three constraints shape how that can happen:

- Customer data must remain in the EU.
- The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.
- Spending this fiscal year is approved up to US$80,000.

The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system and an ISO 9001 quality management system. ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected to start next year, although the start date is not yet fixed.

## Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

At the piloting stage, Tamarack Instruments has enough real AI activity to ground a serious leadership discussion. It is also early enough that governance can be designed in rather than retrofitted. The visual inspection pilot is the right focal point. It sits on a production line, it involves production staff and works council consultation, and it carries the firmest deadline.

Your decision is expected by 15 December 2026. Only a few months separate that date from a Q2 2027 production go-live, so leadership needs agreed ownership, a shared risk position and a sequenced plan quickly rather than a long discovery exercise. A single structured day produces those decisions inside your decision window.

At US$7,500, the Workshop also leaves the large majority of the approved US$80,000 available for whatever follow-on work the leadership team decides is warranted.

Most importantly, the Client does not need to build AI governance from nothing. The ISMS and quality management system already provide management review, internal audit, document control, risk treatment and corrective action. The Workshop positions AI governance as an extension of those structures, so that ISO/IEC 42001 work builds on existing evidence rather than duplicating it.

## Four-Block Structure with Client-Specific Emphasis

The day runs as four 90-minute blocks. It draws on two Academy frameworks, the GRC Workbook and the Slow AI Kitchen, with AI Governance for Boards available as a reference for board-level reporting.

**Block 1, Alignment.** This block opens with the June 2026 internal audit finding and the three AI use cases. The two internal auditors help the room establish an honest picture of current posture. The Head of Data and the CISO address where AI systems and data sit today, including EU data residency for the chatbot. The block closes with a shared statement of governance intent that the whole leadership group can stand behind.

**Block 2, Principles.** The room agrees and ranks the operating principles that will govern AI at Tamarack Instruments. Each of the three Academy principles is applied to your context:

- **Slow AI** asks what must be true before the visual inspection system moves from pilot to production, and how quality acceptance under ISO 9001 should apply to it.
- **Informed Intent** addresses how customers using the support chatbot and staff affected on the German line are told what AI is doing. This includes how works council consultation fits into the sequence.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human** clarifies who signs off on inspection outcomes and who answers for chatbot responses. This matters directly to the General Counsel, the Head of Quality and the German plant manager.

**Block 3, Roadmap.** The room sequences governance work against the EU AI Act, GDPR, the ISO/IEC 42001 target and the existing ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 systems. The draft 12-month roadmap is anchored on three priorities:

- building the AI inventory first,
- reaching production readiness for visual inspection before Q2 2027,
- positioning the ISO/IEC 42001 effort to start from the ISMS and quality management system rather than alongside them.

The planned sales forecasting use case is placed on the roadmap so that it enters through the governance process rather than around it. Every roadmap item leaves the room with a named owner.

**Block 4, Decisions.** The executive decision session addresses four items:

- governance ownership,
- risk appetite for AI,
- oversight cadence, including whether AI is added to existing ISMS and quality management reviews,
- the budget mandate within the approved US$80,000.

The day closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix*, a short teaching fable used as the retention device for the three principles. It gives participants a common shorthand that remains useful well after the session.

## Deliverables

Within the agreed follow-up period, Tamarack Instruments receives three documents:

1. **A decision log** recording each decision, the options considered, the owner and the review date.
2. **A prioritized 12-month governance roadmap** with owners, as agreed in the room.
3. **Written readiness observations** on the Client's current AI governance posture.

The readiness observations are advisory. They are not an audit opinion, and they make no prediction about any certification outcome.

Regulatory discussion during the day is orientation, not legal advice. The roadmap and decision log will flag the following items for confirmation with external counsel:

- how the EU AI Act applies to the visual inspection system and to the support chatbot,
- the GDPR position of any personal data processed by current or planned systems,
- contractual and technical arrangements supporting EU data residency,
- the requirements governing works council consultation in Germany.
