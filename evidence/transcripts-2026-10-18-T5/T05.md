# Proposal: Implementation Accelerator for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The Client is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in Germany and the United States. Its use of AI is at three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded that Tamarack has no inventory of AI systems. Your stated objective is clear: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." The EU AI Act and GDPR are the regulatory drivers. ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected to begin next year, although the start date is not yet fixed.

Three constraints shape the work:

- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.
- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.

Tamarack already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system and an ISO 9001 quality management system. This is a significant advantage. ISO/IEC 42001 shares the harmonized management system structure used by both standards. Tamarack's AI Management System (AIMS) should therefore extend what already works rather than create a parallel bureaucracy. Every artifact in this proposal names the existing system it attaches to.

## 2. Why an Intensive AIMS Foundation Build Fits Now

**Decision timeline.** A decision by 15 December 2026 allows the Implementation Accelerator to run in a five-day window in January 2027. This window is proposed and subject to confirmation. It leaves the Client most of Q1 2027 to adopt artifacts through its own governance, consult the works council and apply controls to the visual inspection pilot before the Q2 2027 production go-live. Delaying the foundation work would compress consultation and adoption into the weeks immediately before go-live.

**Certification target.** The Accelerator structures artifacts for future ISO/IEC 42001 conformity work. Starting now means that when the Client fixes its 42001 start date, it begins from a working foundation and not a blank page. The outcome is readiness. This proposal does not promise certification or any audit result.

**Audit finding.** The inventory built on Day 2 responds directly to the June 2026 finding. It also gives the internal auditors a defined artifact to test.

**Budget fit.** The Implementation Accelerator is priced at US$25,000, within the US$80,000 approved for this fiscal year.

**Regulatory orientation.** The points below are orientation only and do not constitute legal advice.

- **Customer service chatbot.** EU AI Act transparency obligations for systems that interact with people are likely relevant. The chatbot also processes customer personal data under GDPR, and the EU data residency constraint becomes a design requirement in the inventory and vendor policy. *Confirm with external counsel.*
- **Visual inspection.** Classification under the EU AI Act depends on facts not yet known, including the system's role in product quality and safety. Classification will be worked through on Day 2. *Confirm with external counsel.*
- **Works council.** Systems that are capable of monitoring employee behaviour or performance may trigger German co-determination rights. The visual inspection pilot should be assessed for this before production use. *Confirm with external counsel.*
- **Sales forecasting.** This system is planned rather than deployed. It enters the inventory now so that it is governed from design onward.
