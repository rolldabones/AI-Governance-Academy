# Practitioner Academy Proposal for Tamarack Instruments (fictional)

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Practitioner Academy, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** 2 days, live virtual, four 90-minute working sessions per day
**Price:** US$12,500
**Source:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026, with Dana Whitfield, Ravi Okafor and Ingrid Solberg

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States, with about 1,100 staff. The Client describes its AI maturity as Piloting and its AI use as follows:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The Client's stated regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 work is planned to start next year, and that start date is not fixed. In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems.

The Client lists an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function as incumbent frameworks. The extent to which records or controls under those frameworks can be reused for AI governance is Unknown, and the program will not assume any.

## 2. Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The sponsor's stated objective is direct: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." Meeting that objective depends on people inside the Client who can build and maintain governance artifacts themselves, not on a document delivered from outside.

The Practitioner Academy addresses this in three ways:

- **It responds to the audit finding.** Participants start the AI system inventory during the program, which responds directly to the June 2026 finding. This proposal makes no claim about how that finding will be assessed or closed.
- **It works on the Client's own systems.** Participants build in the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) using the Client's own use cases.
- **It brings the right roles into one room.** The audience roles stated for this program are the General Counsel, CISO, Head of Quality, Head of Data, German plant manager and two internal auditors. This mix allows legal, security, quality, data, operations and assurance perspectives to work on the same artifacts at the same time.

The program is anchored in three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

## 3. Running Worked Examples

Two use cases run through both days.

1. **AI visual inspection, German production line.** This is the system named in the sponsor's objective. It also carries a stated constraint: the German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.
2. **Customer service chatbot.** This is the only deployed system. It is the natural test case for GDPR and for the stated constraint that customer data must stay in the EU.

**Sales forecasting** enters the inventory and classification exercise as a planned system, so that the inventory covers all three systems.

**Confidentiality approach.** All exercise material uses sanitized analogs:

- Chatbot exercises use synthetic support transcripts, so no real customer data leaves the EU or enters the program.
- Visual inspection exercises use synthetic defect descriptions and contain no images, records or data relating to production staff.

The exercises design governance for production staff matters but do not touch production staff. Works council consultation is built into each relevant artifact as a required gate. Whether that consultation has taken place is Unknown.
