# Proposal: Implementation Accelerator for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Implementation Accelerator, AI Governance Academy
**Source:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026, with Dana Whitfield, Ravi Okafor and Ingrid Solberg

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. Its headquarters jurisdiction is the Netherlands, and it also operates in Germany and the United States. The Client has about 1,100 staff and stated a revenue band "in the US$200 to 300 million range." That figure was spoken during discovery and has not been taken from filings.

The Client's AI portfolio is at an early stage and is growing:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that the Client has no inventory of AI systems. The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. The Client intends to begin ISO/IEC 42001 (https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) work next year, and the start date is not yet fixed.

The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 (https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) information security management system, an ISO 9001 (https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html) quality management system and an internal audit function. The stated objective is direct: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

Three constraints shape the work:

1. Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
2. Customer data must stay in the EU.
3. The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

## 2. Why an Intensive AIMS Foundation Build Fits Now

A decision is expected by 15 December 2026. The stated production objective for visual inspection is Q2 2027. A five-day intensive gives the Client a working Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) foundation well ahead of that objective. It leaves time for the Client's own review and adoption cycles, and for any works council consultation, before a production go-live.

The approach also suits the Client's position in three ways.

**It addresses the audit finding at its source.** The absence of an AI inventory is the gap internal audit recorded. Day 2 builds that inventory with a classification scheme, and the remaining artifacts depend on it. Whether the finding is closed remains a determination for the Client's internal audit function.

**It builds on what the Client already runs.** The Client operates management systems under ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001. Each artifact is therefore designed to attach to those systems rather than stand apart from them. The specific registers, procedures and document control arrangements within those systems are not described in the discovery material, so each attachment point is `Unknown` until the Client confirms it during the week.

**It prepares for ISO/IEC 42001 without getting ahead of it.** The artifacts are structured for future conformity work under ISO/IEC 42001, so next year's effort starts from a built foundation. The Accelerator delivers readiness. It does not deliver, and this proposal does not promise, certification or any audit result.

The Accelerator is a deliberate, paced build. It reflects three working principles:

- **Slow AI:** governance is designed before scale.
- **Informed Intent:** every AI use is recorded with its purpose and its owner.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** decision rights and accountability are named for people, not systems.

## 3. The Five-Day Build Spine, Tailored to the Client

The sequence follows two dependencies. The inventory comes before the risk register, and the charter comes before the policies.

**Day 1: Governance charter and roles.**
- Draft the AI governance charter, covering purpose, scope, roles, decision rights and meeting cadence.
- Map the charter against the Client's existing ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 governance structures. The specific attachment points are `Unknown` pending Client confirmation.
- Hold the executive checkpoint to confirm scope and decision rights.

**Day 2: AI system inventory and classification.**
- Build the inventory schema.
- Enter the three known use cases as first entries: the deployed customer service chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned sales forecasting system.
- Apply a classification scheme that orients each system to the EU AI Act and GDPR. This classification is orientation only; confirm with external counsel.

**Day 3: Risk and impact assessment plus risk register.**
- Define the AI risk and impact assessment method.
- Seed the risk register from the inventory and the June 2026 audit finding. All seeded risk statements are drafts for Client validation.
- Draft an impact assessment approach for the visual inspection pilot. Any step that touches production staff is held for works council consultation, as the Client's constraint requires.

**Day 4: Core policies and operating procedures.**
- Draft the AI acceptable use, AI lifecycle governance, vendor and supplier governance, AI incident response and AI change management policies, with supporting operating procedures.
- Write the EU data residency requirement for customer data into the vendor and supplier governance policy and the lifecycle governance policy as a stated control requirement.

**Day 5: Evidence model, implementation priorities and executive readout.**
- Build the evidence model, mapping each artifact to the assurance question it answers. This lets internal audit and future ISO/IEC 42001 readiness work test it.
- Set implementation priorities for the next 90 days.
- Deliver the executive readout.

The working method draws on the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen). Board-facing material references AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards).
