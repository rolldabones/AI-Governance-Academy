## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States. The Client has three AI use cases at different stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The Client describes its AI maturity as Piloting. Its stated regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is expected to start next year, although the start date is not fixed.

An internal audit finding from June 2026 recorded that no inventory of AI systems exists. Your stated objective is clear: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

Three constraints shape this proposal:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

The Client lists ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 as incumbent frameworks. This proposal does not assume any register, process or control exists under either one. Participants are asked to bring whatever material they hold so that the new artifacts can be aligned with it.

## 2. Why Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The audit finding and the Q2 2027 production target point to the same need. People inside the Client must be able to build and maintain governance artifacts themselves rather than receive them from outside.

The Practitioner Academy places the stated audience in the working repositories for two days. That audience is the General Counsel, CISO, Head of Quality, Head of Data, German plant manager and two internal auditors. Participants leave having started the artifacts the audit finding and the production target call for.

The program rests on three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human. These suit a pilot-stage organization that wants deliberate decisions before a system reaches production.

The program does not promise ISO/IEC 42001 certification or any audit outcome. It builds the capability and the starting artifacts that later work can draw on.

## 3. Running Worked Examples

Three AI use cases serve as the working material across both days:

1. **AI visual inspection** is the primary example. It is the system named in your production objective. It also raises the EU AI Act classification questions and the works council consultation requirement.
2. **Customer service chatbot** is the second example. It is already deployed, so it shows governance applied to a live system. It also engages the GDPR and EU data residency constraints.
3. **AI sales forecasting** is used only in the inventory and classification session. This shows how a planned system enters governance before it is built.

**How the constraints are respected:**

- **No real customer data.** No customer data is used at any point. Chatbot exercises use synthetic support tickets written in session.
- **No real production material.** Visual inspection exercises use synthetic or publicly available defect imagery. They use no production line data and no images or information about production staff.
- **Works council consultation.** Any artifact that would touch production staff is marked for German works council consultation before use. Nothing produced in the program is deployed.
