# Proposal: Practitioner Academy for Tamarack Instruments

**To:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Practitioner Academy, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** 2 days, live virtual, four 90-minute working sessions per day
**Price:** US$12,500

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage. It has three AI use cases:

* **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
* **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
* **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The Client's regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is to start next year, and the start date is not fixed. The Client's incumbent frameworks are an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

## 2. Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The Practitioner Academy does not hand the Client a governance program. It builds the capability to produce one, using the Client's own AI use cases as the working material. Four facts make the timing relevant:

* The June 2026 finding names a specific missing artifact. Starting the inventory is a Day 1 exercise.
* The Q2 2027 production date for visual inspection sets a horizon for governance on a named system.
* The chatbot is deployed, so its governance is current work rather than planning.
* ISO/IEC 42001 work is expected next year, and practitioners who already work in structured repositories can carry that work forward.

The program teaches three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent and Final Liability rests with the Human. Participants work directly in two repositories:

* GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
* Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

Two further repositories serve as references:

* AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards), for the board layer.
* The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done), for the acceptance layer.

## 3. Running Worked Examples

Two use cases run through both days. The third enters on Day 1 only.

* **AI visual inspection (piloting).** This is the system named in the stated objective. Its link to production staff brings in the constraint that the German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff. That constraint makes the oversight and workflow design concrete.
* **Customer service chatbot (deployed).** This is the only system in use. The constraint that customer data must stay in the EU, together with GDPR, gives the governed prompting and evidence exercises real boundaries.
* **AI sales forecasting (planned).** This system enters the inventory and classification exercise only. It tests the intake procedure on a system before it is built.

**Confidentiality approach.** No real customer data is used in any exercise. Chatbot exercises use a sanitized analog: synthetic support queries written for the session. Visual inspection exercises use a sanitized process description with no images, names or data relating to production staff.

* **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
