# Executive Workshop Proposal: Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Executive Workshop, AI Governance Academy

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage.

The Client's AI portfolio has three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems.

**Regulatory and certification context**

- The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR.
- Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is to start next year. The start date is not fixed.

**Existing frameworks**

- An ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system.
- An ISO 9001 quality management system.
- An internal audit function.

**Constraints**

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

**Objective.** Dana Whitfield has stated it directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

**Timeline.** A decision is expected by 15 December 2026.

## 2. Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

At the piloting stage, the governance questions are still open, and they are still inexpensive to answer:

- who owns AI outcomes
- how much AI risk the Client will accept
- how often leadership reviews AI
- what mandate the governance work carries

A single day puts these questions to the people who hold the decision rights. It produces a recorded answer instead of a series of separate conversations.

The day has three anchors:

- the stated Q2 2027 objective
- the June 2026 audit finding
- the expected decision date of 15 December 2026

Together they make the workshop a starting point for sequenced work, not an awareness session. Where the workshop date should sit relative to 15 December 2026 is a question to confirm at intake.

The Client already runs ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 9001 and an internal audit function. The workshop therefore treats AI governance as an extension of these frameworks, not a parallel system. ISO/IEC 42001 follows the same harmonized management system structure as ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001, which supports that approach.

The specific registers, processes and records within the ISMS and quality management system that AI governance would attach to are currently `Unknown`. They will be confirmed at intake.

## 3. Four-Block Structure with Client-Specific Emphasis

The day runs as four 90-minute blocks with breaks. It draws on two frameworks:

- the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

It also references two further resources:

- AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards), for the board layer
- The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done), for the acceptance layer

### Block 1: Alignment

**What the room does:** Reviews current AI posture across the deployed chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned sales forecasting. The June 2026 finding on the missing AI inventory is the reference point.

**Output:** A shared statement of governance intent.

### Block 2: Principles

**What the room does:** Agrees governance operating principles. These include the three Academy principles, applied to the Client's three use cases:

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

**Output:** Principles ranked by the room.

### Block 3: Roadmap

**What the room does:** Sequences governance work against four inputs:

- the EU AI Act
- GDPR
- the ISO/IEC 42001 target
- the existing ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 9001 and internal audit frameworks

The roadmap also accounts for:

- the Q2 2027 objective
- the requirement that customer data stays in the EU
- works council consultation, where it applies

Whether the visual inspection pilot falls within works council consultation is a question to confirm at intake.

**Output:** A draft 12-month roadmap with owners.

### Block 4: Decisions

**What the room does:** Records executive decisions on four topics:

- governance ownership
- risk appetite for AI
- oversight cadence
- budget mandate

**Closing exercise:** The block closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix* ([facilitation kit](https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-Governance-Academy/blob/main/the-turtle-and-the-phoenix.md)). The kit's twenty-minute closing exercise serves as the retention device for the three principles.

**Output:** A decision log.
