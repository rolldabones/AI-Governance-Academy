# Practitioner Academy Proposal: Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel and budget holder
**Program:** AI Governance Academy, Practitioner Academy | 2 days, live virtual | US$12,500

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The Client is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States. Its AI portfolio sits at three stages:

* A customer service chatbot is deployed on the support portal.
* AI visual inspection is being piloted on the German production line.
* AI sales forecasting is planned for next year.

The Client's AI maturity is at the piloting stage. Its regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, and the start date is not yet fixed. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. The Client's incumbent frameworks are ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 9001 and an internal audit function.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

## 2. Why Hands-on Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The stated objective sets a fixed horizon, and the June 2026 finding names a specific gap. Three systems at three lifecycle stages give the Client a complete set of working material: one in operation, one in pilot and one in planning. The intended audience covers legal, security, quality, data, plant operations and internal audit. These are the roles that must agree on who owns each AI outcome.

The program will have these practitioners build governance artifacts themselves, working directly in two repositories:

* GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
* Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

Participants leave with started artifacts and the ability to continue unaided. The program does not promise any ISO/IEC 42001 certification outcome or any audit result.

Three principles anchor the work:

* **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
* **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
* **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

## 3. Running Worked Examples and Confidentiality

**Selection.** The program will use two running examples across both days. Sales forecasting enters on Day 1 as a planned system.

1. **Customer service chatbot.** As the deployed system, it exercises customer data handling under GDPR and the EU AI Act together.
2. **AI visual inspection.** It is the system named in the stated objective. It also brings in quality, plant operations and works council considerations.
3. **AI sales forecasting.** It is used in inventory and classification so participants practice governing a system before it is built.

**Constraints.**

* **Customer data must stay in the EU.** No customer data will be used in any session. Chatbot exercises use synthetic support conversations written for the program.
* **German works council consultation.** The works council is consulted before anything touches production staff. Visual inspection exercises use a sanitized analog: a generic inspection scenario with no production line data and no data about production staff. All workflows drafted in the program are paper designs. Any design that would affect production staff goes through works council consultation before use.
