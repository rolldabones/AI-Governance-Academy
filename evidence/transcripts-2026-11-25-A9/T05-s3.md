# Proposal: Implementation Accelerator for Tamarack Instruments

**AI Governance Academy | Prepared for Dana Whitfield, General Counsel**

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States, with about 1,100 staff. The Client's AI portfolio has three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded no inventory of AI systems. The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, and its start date is not yet fixed. The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function.

The Client has three stated constraints:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

## 2. Why an Intensive AIMS Foundation Build Fits

A decision is expected by 15 December 2026. The stated objective points to Q2 2027, and ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year. Together these leave a short window to establish an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) foundation before the visual inspection objective date and before conformity work begins.

The Implementation Accelerator is a five-day intensive working session. Your build team produces the core governance artifacts together, in one sequence. The program does not start from a blank page, and it does not run alongside the Client's existing management systems. Each artifact is designed to attach to the ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, the ISO 9001 quality management system and the internal audit function. Exactly where each artifact attaches is `Unknown` at this stage and is listed as a question to confirm at intake.

All artifacts are structured for future ISO/IEC 42001 conformity work. The program builds readiness. It does not promise certification, audit results or any SOC 2 (System and Organization Controls 2) outcome.

The work is grounded in three principles:

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

The build draws on two working frameworks:

- GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

The board-layer reference is AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards).

## 3. The Five-Day Build Spine, Tailored to Tamarack Instruments

The days are sequenced around two dependencies. The inventory comes before the risk register, and the charter comes before the policies.

**Day 1: Governance charter and roles.**
- The team drafts the AI governance charter: purpose, scope, roles, decision rights and meeting cadence.
- Each AI system is assigned a named human owner, consistent with Final Liability rests with the Human.
- The charter is designed to sit alongside the governance of the existing ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 management systems. Its precise attachment point is `Unknown` until intake.
- Executive checkpoint: the charter direction is confirmed.

**Day 2: AI system inventory and classification.**
- The team builds the inventory schema and enters the three known use cases as the first records: the customer service chatbot, the AI visual inspection pilot and the planned AI sales forecasting system.
- This work responds directly to the June 2026 internal audit finding.
- Classification fields include an EU AI Act orientation for each system. This is orientation only, and any classification with legal effect carries the note: confirm with external counsel.

**Day 3: Risk and impact assessment plus risk register.**
- The team applies the risk and impact assessment method to each inventoried system and builds the AI risk register.
- Seeded risk statements are labeled as drafts for Client validation.
- The assessment considers the stated constraint that customer data stays in the EU, along with the GDPR implications for the chatbot. Items with legal effect carry the note: confirm with external counsel.
- For visual inspection, the assessment records the works council consultation requirement as a dependency for any production deployment. The required consultation path is a matter to confirm with external counsel.
