# Executive Workshop Proposal: Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Executive Workshop, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** One day, live virtual, four 90-minute blocks with breaks
**Price:** US$7,500

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The Client is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States.

The Client's AI use sits at three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded no inventory of AI systems. The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, and its start date is not fixed.

Your stated objective sets the frame for the day: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

The Client's stated constraints are:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

## 2. Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

The Client's AI maturity is Piloting. At this stage the main open questions are leadership decisions rather than technical build work:

- who owns AI governance
- what risk the Client will accept from AI
- how often leadership reviews it
- what budget it carries

A single day lets executives settle these questions together while the visual inspection system is still a pilot. That is before production use, ISO/IEC 42001 work and the planned sales forecasting add further commitments.

A decision is expected by 15 December 2026. How the Workshop date should sit relative to that date is a question for intake.

## 3. Four-Block Structure with Client-Specific Emphasis

The Workshop draws on the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen). It uses AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) as the board-layer reference and The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done) as the acceptance-layer reference.

**Block 1: Alignment.** The room reviews the three use cases at their stated stages and the June 2026 audit finding of no AI system inventory. The block produces a shared statement of governance intent, anchored to the Q2 2027 production objective for visual inspection.

**Block 2: Principles.** The room applies the three Academy principles to the Client's use cases:

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit. The room tests the visual inspection pilot against this before production.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions. The room applies this to the deployed chatbot and the planned sales forecasting. Whether an authorization record exists for the chatbot is `Unknown`.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene. The room uses this to frame ownership for each use case.

The block produces operating principles ranked by the room.

**Block 3: Roadmap.** The room sequences governance work against three inputs:

- **Regulation:** the EU AI Act and GDPR. This content is orientation, not legal advice. How the EU AI Act classifies each use case, and how GDPR applies to each, requires confirmation with external counsel.
- **Certification:** ISO/IEC 42001 as the certification target. The Workshop makes no certification or audit outcome promise.
- **Incumbent frameworks:** AI governance is positioned as an extension of the Client's ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, ISO 9001 quality management system and internal audit function, not as a parallel structure. The specific registers, processes and controls within these frameworks where AI governance would attach are `Unknown` and will be confirmed at intake.

An AI system inventory is proposed as early roadmap work in response to the audit finding. The block produces a draft 12-month roadmap with owners.

**Block 4: Decisions.** Executives record decisions on four items:

- governance ownership
- risk appetite for AI
- oversight cadence
- budget mandate

The block closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix* ([facilitation kit](https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-Governance-Academy/blob/main/the-turtle-and-the-phoenix.md)) and the kit's twenty-minute closing exercise. This is the retention device for the three principles.
