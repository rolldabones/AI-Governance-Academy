## 2. The Case for Continuous Fractional Advisory at This Stage

At the Piloting stage, one system is already deployed, one is approaching production and one is planned. Governance therefore has to keep pace with a series of transitions rather than a single launch. Each transition creates its own decisions:

- who authorizes the system
- on what terms it operates
- who owns its outcomes
- what evidence will later support an ISO/IEC 42001 readiness assessment

A one-time assessment would capture the position at a single point. Continuous fractional advisory keeps senior governance judgment available as the visual inspection pilot moves toward production, as ISO/IEC 42001 work begins and as sales forecasting is planned. It does this without adding headcount.

The engagement applies three principles throughout:

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

The program uses two working frameworks:

- the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

Board-level reporting draws on AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards).
