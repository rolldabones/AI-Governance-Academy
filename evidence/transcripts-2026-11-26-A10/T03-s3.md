# Executive Workshop Proposal

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel, Tamarack Instruments
**Program:** Executive Workshop, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** One day, live virtual | **Price:** US$7,500

Dana Whitfield,

This proposal sets out how a one-day Executive Workshop would help Tamarack Instruments align its leadership, agree governance principles, draft a roadmap and record executive decisions on AI.

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff.

The Client's AI use is at three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded no inventory of AI systems.

The Client names the following drivers and frameworks:

- **Regulatory drivers:** the EU AI Act and GDPR.
- **Certification target:** ISO/IEC 42001, with work to start next year. The start date is not fixed.
- **Incumbent frameworks:** an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function.

The Client's constraints are:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

Your stated objective is: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

A decision is expected by 15 December 2026.

## 2. Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

Tamarack Instruments is at the piloting stage of AI maturity. One system is deployed, one is in pilot and one is planned. At this stage, decisions on ownership, risk appetite and oversight can still shape how the visual inspection pilot moves toward production, rather than being fitted afterward.

A single day gives the leadership group four structured blocks. Together they produce a decision log and a draft roadmap well ahead of the Q2 2027 objective.

With a decision expected by 15 December 2026, the Workshop can be scheduled before that date. Whether the Workshop record should feed into that decision is a question to confirm at intake.

The Workshop treats AI governance as an extension of the ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, the ISO 9001 quality management system and the internal audit function, not as a parallel structure. Which registers, procedures or records within those frameworks AI governance would attach to is `Unknown` and will be confirmed at intake.

## 3. Workshop Structure and Client-Specific Emphasis

The Workshop draws on two frameworks:

- the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
- the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

It is anchored in three Academy principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent and Final Liability rests with the Human.

**Block 1, Alignment (90 minutes)**
- The room reviews the Client's AI posture across the chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned sales forecasting.
- The June 2026 internal audit finding is the starting fact.
- Using the GRC Workbook domains of governance structures, inventories, risk management, policies and evidence, participants list the AI systems known to them as a starting point for an inventory.
- **Output:** a shared statement of governance intent.

**Block 2, Principles (90 minutes)**
The room applies each principle to the Client's use cases:

- **Slow AI** holds that AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit. The room tests the visual inspection pilot against these three tests ahead of the Q2 2027 objective.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions. The room examines what that authorization would state for the chatbot and the planned forecasting.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene. The room identifies where named owners are needed for each use case.

**Output:** governance operating principles agreed and ranked by the room.

**Block 3, Roadmap (90 minutes)**
The room sequences governance work against:

- the EU AI Act and GDPR
- ISO/IEC 42001, whose start date is not fixed
- the incumbent ISMS, quality management system and internal audit function

The Q2 2027 objective is treated as a milestone. The EU data residency and works council constraints are treated as sequencing conditions.

The Definition of Done (https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done) informs acceptance criteria for roadmap items.

Regulatory content in this block is orientation, not legal advice. Items touching the EU AI Act or GDPR are flagged for confirmation with external counsel.

**Output:** a draft 12-month roadmap with owners.

**Block 4, Decisions (90 minutes)**
The executive decision session covers:

- governance ownership
- risk appetite for AI
- oversight cadence
- budget mandate

AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) informs how decisions are framed for reporting upward.

The block closes with *The Turtle and the Phoenix* ([facilitation kit](https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-Governance-Academy/blob/main/the-turtle-and-the-phoenix.md)) and the kit's twenty-minute closing exercise. This slot is the retention device for the three principles.

**Output:** a decision log.

## 4. Deliverables

- **Decision log:** decisions on governance ownership, risk appetite for AI, oversight cadence and budget mandate, with owners and review dates.
- **Prioritized roadmap:** the draft 12-month roadmap as agreed in Block 3, with regulatory items flagged for external counsel.
- **Readiness observations:** observations drawn from the Workshop record. They are not an assessment of conformity and do not predict any ISO/IEC 42001 certification or audit outcome.
