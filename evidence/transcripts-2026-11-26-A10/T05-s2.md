## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It has about 1,100 staff and is headquartered in the Netherlands, with operations in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States.

The Client's AI portfolio spans three stages:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems.

The Client already operates three structures that AI governance can attach to:

- an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system;
- an ISO 9001 quality management system;
- an internal audit function.

Three constraints shape the work:

- customer data must stay in the EU;
- the German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff;
- a budget of up to US$80,000 is approved for this fiscal year.

The stated objective is: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." (Dana Whitfield)

## 2. Why an Intensive AIMS Foundation Build Fits Now

A decision is expected by 15 December 2026. The stated objective points to Q2 2027 for visual inspection going into production. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, with the start date not yet fixed.

These three dates favor a short, concentrated build over an extended program. The Implementation Accelerator produces the foundation of an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) in one working week. Several of these foundation artifacts are typically needed before production decisions can be governed:

- a governance charter;
- an AI system inventory;
- a risk register;
- core policies;
- an evidence model.

Building them early leaves the months before Q2 2027 for validation, adoption and operation rather than first drafting.

**ISO/IEC 42001 readiness, not certification.** All artifacts are structured for future ISO/IEC 42001 conformity work. The Accelerator builds readiness. It does not promise certification, audit results or any external assurance outcome.

**Attachment to the Client's existing management systems.** Every artifact is designed to attach to the Client's ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system, ISO 9001 quality management system and internal audit function rather than stand alone. The specific attachment points inside those systems are `Unknown` at this stage and are listed as intake questions in Section 7. One attachment point is known: the AI system inventory responds directly to the June 2026 internal audit finding.

**Guiding principles.** The build is guided by three principles:

- Slow AI holds that "AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit."
- Informed Intent holds that "no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions."
- Final Liability rests with the Human holds that "every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene."

**Frameworks.** The working frameworks are the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen). The executive readout draws on AI Governance for Boards (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards).
