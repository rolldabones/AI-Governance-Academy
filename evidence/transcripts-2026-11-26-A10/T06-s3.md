# Guided AI GRC Build: Proposal for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Guided AI GRC Build, twelve weeks, US$60,000

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States, with about 1,100 staff. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage, and it has three AI use cases:

- A customer service chatbot is deployed on its support portal.
- AI visual inspection is being piloted on its German production line.
- AI sales forecasting is planned for next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. ISO/IEC 42001 is the certification target, with work to start next year. That start date is not yet fixed.

Dana Whitfield stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." Three constraints apply:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

The incumbent frameworks are an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function. A decision is expected by 15 December 2026.

## 2. The Case for a Guided Build

A consultant-delivered AIMS can produce documents quickly. However, the people who must run the system have not built it, so they are often unable to explain or defend it. The Guided AI GRC Build reverses that arrangement. The Tamarack Instruments build team produces every artifact, guided by weekly workshops, coaching and structured asynchronous work. At Week 12 the Client's team owns the system, understands why each part exists and can operate it without depending on an outside firm.

The program applies three principles:

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

The program uses two open frameworks:

- **GRC Workbook** (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) provides the governance, risk and compliance structure.
- **Slow AI Kitchen** (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) provides the governed workflow and human oversight patterns used in Week 9.
