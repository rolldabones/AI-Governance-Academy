# Guided AI GRC Build

**Proposal for Tamarack Instruments**
Prepared for Dana Whitfield, General Counsel

## 1. Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The Client is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States. It has about 1,100 staff, and its AI maturity is at the piloting stage.

Three AI use cases are in scope:

- A customer service chatbot deployed on the support portal.
- An AI visual inspection pilot on the German production line.
- AI sales forecasting planned for next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. ISO/IEC 42001 work is to start next year, and its start date is not yet fixed.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." Three constraints apply:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

This proposal sets out a twelve-week guided build. It produces a functioning minimum viable Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) anchored on those three use cases. It responds to the June 2026 finding and organizes the work around ISO/IEC 42001 readiness themes.

## 2. The Case for a Guided Build

A conventional consulting engagement would deliver a finished governance pack and then leave. The Guided AI GRC Build works the other way. The Client's own team builds each artifact during the program, with weekly workshops, coaching and structured asynchronous work between sessions. At Week 12 the people who will operate the system are the people who wrote it. They understand why each control exists and can maintain it without depending on an outside party. This matters here for three reasons:

- **Inventory finding.** The inventory gap was raised as an internal audit finding, so the response has to be something the Client owns and can evidence over time.
- **Continuation into ISO/IEC 42001.** ISO/IEC 42001 work is planned for next year, and a system the Client already operates is a stronger starting point than documents it must first learn.
- **Operational depth.** Governing visual inspection on a production line requires judgment from quality, data, security and plant perspectives working together, and that judgment cannot be transferred in a handover file.

The program rests on three principles:

- **Slow AI** holds that AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent** holds that no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human** holds that every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

The program uses three frameworks:

- **GRC Workbook** (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) for the management system artifacts.
- **Slow AI Kitchen** (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) for governed workflows and human oversight.
- **AI Governance for Boards** (https://github.com/rolldabones/ai-governance-for-boards) as the reference for steering and board-layer reporting.
