# Practitioner Academy Proposal: Tamarack Instruments (fictional)

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Practitioner Academy, AI Governance Academy
**Format:** 2 days, live virtual, four 90-minute working sessions per day
**Price:** US$12,500

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The Client is headquartered in the Netherlands, operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has about 1,100 staff. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage.

The Client has three AI use cases:

- **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
- **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
- **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is planned to start next year, and the start date is not fixed. The incumbent frameworks are an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function. An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded no inventory of AI systems.

Dana Whitfield has stated the objective: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

Three constraints shape this design:

- Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
- Customer data must stay in the EU.
- The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.

## Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The Practitioner Academy is built around participants doing the work themselves. Over two days, the General Counsel, CISO, Head of Quality, Head of Data, German plant manager and two internal auditors work directly in the GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) and the Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen). They leave with artifacts already started and know where each instrument lives.

The timing aligns with three dated facts:

- The June 2026 finding concerns an AI system inventory, and Session 2 starts one.
- The Q2 2027 objective centers on visual inspection, which runs through both days as a worked example.
- ISO/IEC 42001 work is planned for next year. Artifacts started in the program can feed that work, though the program makes no certification or audit outcome promise.

The program teaches three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent and Final Liability rests with the Human.

- **Slow AI:** AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
- **Informed Intent:** no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
- **Final Liability rests with the Human:** every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

## Running Worked Examples

**The customer service chatbot and AI visual inspection are the two running examples. AI sales forecasting is a third, lighter example.**

- **Chatbot:** It is the one deployed system. It lets participants work the EU customer data constraint and GDPR through sanitized analogs.
- **Visual inspection:** It is tied directly to the stated Q2 2027 objective. It also brings the works council constraint into oversight design.
- **Sales forecasting:** Because it is planned, participants can practice inventory entry and new-system intake before any build.
