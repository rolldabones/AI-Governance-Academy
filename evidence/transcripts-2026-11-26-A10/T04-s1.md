# Practitioner Academy Proposal

## 1. Situation Summary

Dana Whitfield, Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. The company has about 1,100 staff, is headquartered in the Netherlands and operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States. Its AI maturity is at the piloting stage. It has three AI use cases:

* **Deployed:** a customer service chatbot on the support portal.
* **Piloting:** AI visual inspection on the German production line.
* **Planned:** AI sales forecasting next year.

The regulatory drivers are the EU AI Act and GDPR. Work toward ISO/IEC 42001 is set to start next year, although the start date is not fixed. The incumbent frameworks are an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system, an ISO 9001 quality management system and an internal audit function.

In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded that no inventory of AI systems exists. You have stated the objective directly: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027."

Three constraints shape the program:

* Customer data must stay in the EU.
* The German works council is consulted before anything touches production staff.
* Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.

## 2. Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The audit finding and the Q2 2027 objective both point to work that practitioners must do: build an inventory, assess risk and define who authorizes and oversees each system. The Practitioner Academy does that work in the room. Participants work directly in two repositories:

* GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)
* Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen)

At the end of the two days, participants leave with started artifacts and know where each one lives.

Three principles anchor the program:

* **Slow AI** holds that AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.
* **Informed Intent** holds that no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.
* **Final Liability rests with the Human** holds that every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

The program is designed for the following roles:

* General Counsel
* CISO
* Head of Quality
* Head of Data
* German plant manager
* Two internal auditors

The program does not confer ISO/IEC 42001 certification and does not promise any audit outcome. It builds the practitioner capability and draft artifacts on which later certification work can draw.

## 3. Running Worked Examples

We propose three of the Client's use cases as working material across both days:

1. **AI visual inspection.** This is the primary example because it is tied directly to the Q2 2027 objective and is the use case where the works council constraint applies.
2. **Customer service chatbot.** This is the deployed system, and it tests governed prompting and the requirement that customer data stays in the EU.
3. **AI sales forecasting.** This is used only in inventory, classification and risk entries. Bringing a planned system into scope before deployment shows the inventory working as an intake control.

**Sanitized analogs.** Exercises use sanitized analogs throughout:

* Synthetic support conversations stand in for chatbot data.
* Synthetic defect descriptions stand in for inspection data.
* No customer data and no information about production staff enters the repositories or any shared environment.

Any drafted workflow that would affect production staff carries works council consultation as a precondition to use.
