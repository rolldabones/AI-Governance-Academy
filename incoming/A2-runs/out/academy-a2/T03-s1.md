# Proposal: Executive Workshop for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Program:** Executive Workshop, AI Governance Academy
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments. It operates in the Netherlands, Germany and the United States and has its headquarters in the Netherlands. The Client has one AI system deployed, one in pilot and one planned. The deployed system is a customer service chatbot on the support portal. The pilot is AI visual inspection on the German production line. The planned system is AI sales forecasting next year. The Client describes its AI maturity as piloting.

An internal audit finding in June 2026 recorded that the Client has no inventory of AI systems. The stated objective is "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." The Profile Block names the EU AI Act and GDPR as regulatory drivers and ISO/IEC 42001 as a certification target. Work on ISO/IEC 42001 is expected to start next year, but the start date is not fixed. The Client already operates an ISO/IEC 27001 information security management system and an ISO 9001 quality management system.

Three constraints apply:

* The approved budget is up to US$80,000 this fiscal year.
* Customer data must stay in the EU.
* The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

## Why a One-Day Executive Session Fits

A piloting organization is at the point where governance choices are still inexpensive to make and costly to reverse later. The visual inspection pilot has not yet moved into production. The sales forecasting system has not yet been built. Decisions made now about ownership, risk appetite and oversight can shape both systems from the start rather than being fitted afterwards.

The Profile Block records a decision expected by 15 December 2026. The subject of that decision and the workshop date are `Unknown`. A single day held before that date would give leadership a shared record to decide from. Because the Q2 2027 objective is fixed and the ISO/IEC 42001 start date is not, the Client needs a roadmap that sequences work against the objective while leaving room for the certification timeline to settle.

The Client already runs ISMS and QMS disciplines. The workshop will therefore treat AI governance as an extension of those systems rather than a parallel structure. The Profile Block names the frameworks but does not describe the registers, processes or records inside them. Specific attachment points are rendered `Unknown` until the Client confirms them. Examples include an asset inventory, a risk register, a nonconformity process and a management review record.

## The Four Blocks

The day runs as four 90-minute blocks with breaks. It draws on two frameworks:

* The GRC Workbook (https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) provides the governance, inventory, risk, policy and evidence domains.
* The Slow AI Kitchen (https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) provides the discipline of deliberate, staged adoption.

**Block 1, Alignment.** The room reviews the Client's current AI posture against the three use cases and the June 2026 audit finding. The missing inventory is the first concrete gap the session addresses. The block produces a shared statement of governance intent.

**Block 2, Principles.** The room agrees governance operating principles, including the three Academy principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human. Each principle is applied to the Client's context.

* **Slow AI** applies to the path from pilot to production for visual inspection.
* **Informed Intent** applies to what leadership needs to know before approving a system.
* **Final Liability rests with the Human** addresses where accountability sits when an AI system produces an output that people act on.

The room ranks the agreed principles.

**Block 3, Roadmap.** The room sequences governance work against four inputs: the EU AI Act, GDPR, the ISO/IEC 42001 target and the existing ISMS and QMS.

* How the EU AI Act classifies each of the three use cases is `Unknown` and must be confirmed with external counsel.
* GDPR obligations for each system, and how the EU data residency constraint applies to each system, should also be confirmed with external counsel.

Regulatory content in this session is orientation, not legal advice. The Profile Block does not state whether the visual inspection pilot touches production staff. That relation is `Unknown`. The roadmap will therefore include a step to determine whether works council consultation applies before production. The block produces a draft 12-month roadmap with owners.

**Block 4, Decisions.** Leadership records decisions on four topics: governance ownership, risk appetite for AI, oversight cadence and budget mandate. The budget mandate discussion will take the approved US$80,000 ceiling as a stated constraint.

Two references support this block:
