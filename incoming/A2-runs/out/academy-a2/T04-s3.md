# Proposal: Practitioner Academy for Tamarack Instruments

**Prepared for:** Dana Whitfield, General Counsel
**Basis:** Discovery call notes of 6 October 2026
**Decision expected:** 15 December 2026

## Situation Summary

Tamarack Instruments designs and manufactures laboratory measurement instruments, with headquarters in the Netherlands and operations in Germany and the United States. The Client has one deployed AI system (a customer service chatbot on the support portal), one pilot (AI visual inspection on the German production line) and one planned system (AI sales forecasting next year). The Client identifies the EU AI Act and GDPR as regulatory drivers. It intends to begin ISO/IEC 42001 work next year, though the start date is not fixed.

In June 2026 an internal audit finding recorded that there is no inventory of AI systems. Your stated objective is clear: "I want governance in place before visual inspection goes into production in Q2 2027." Three constraints shape the work. Budget is approved up to US$80,000 this fiscal year. Customer data must stay in the EU. The German works council must be consulted before anything touches production staff.

## Why Hands-On Practitioner Capability Fits Now

The gap named in the audit finding is practical, not conceptual. An inventory, a classification decision and a risk record must be built by the people who know the systems. The Practitioner Academy puts the audience roles identified in discovery into the same working sessions: General Counsel, CISO, Head of Quality, Head of Data, German plant manager and two internal auditors. Together they produce first versions of these artifacts in the GRC Workbook and Slow AI Kitchen repositories. Each participant leaves knowing where every artifact lives and how to continue it without outside help.

Timing matters. Running the program in early 2027 would leave the Client a working period before the Q2 2027 production target in which to finish and test what the program starts. The Client names ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 9001 as incumbent frameworks. Participants may bring any related materials they hold, and the program will show where workbook artifacts can sit alongside them. The program makes no assumption about what those management systems currently contain.

The program is anchored in three principles: Slow AI, Informed Intent, Final Liability rests with the Human.

## Running Worked Examples

Three of the Client's own use cases carry through both days.

- **Visual inspection (piloting).** This system is tied directly to your Q2 2027 objective. It carries the heaviest risk, oversight and evidence work, and it brings the works council consultation requirement into the design discussion.
- **Customer service chatbot (deployed).** This system is already live. It exercises GDPR considerations, EU data residency and AI Act transparency questions on a running system.
- **Sales forecasting (planned).** This system is used in the inventory and classification session to show how a planned system enters governance before it is built.

The program does not predetermine how any system classifies under the EU AI Act. Participants reach and record those determinations themselves, with their reasoning.

## Two-Day Curriculum

The program runs as two live virtual days. Each day contains four 90-minute working sessions.

**Day 1: GRC Workbook**

1. **Governance structures.** Draft decision rights and roles for AI governance at Tamarack. This session also includes *The Turtle and the Phoenix*, told from the facilitation kit to seat the doctrine before the instruments arrive. The kit's handle map links the story to the workbook modules, and the session closes with the kit's twenty-minute closing exercise.
2. **AI system inventories and classification.** Enter the chatbot, the visual inspection pilot and the planned forecasting system as the first inventory records, plus any further systems participants bring. Draft a classification rationale for each.
3. **Risk and impact assessments.** Write draft risk register entries for visual inspection and the chatbot.
4. **Policies and operating procedures.** Outline the procedures the risk entries call for, beginning with visual inspection.

**Day 2: Slow AI Kitchen and Assurance**

5. **Governed prompting.** Apply governed prompting patterns to chatbot response handling.
6. **Practical AI workflow design.** Design governed workflows for chatbot escalation and visual inspection disposition decisions.
7. **Human oversight and accountability.** Define review points, override authority and named accountability in each workflow. Owners are recorded by the Client during the session; none are assumed in advance.
8. **Evidence collection and audit readiness.** Map the evidence each workflow should generate, using The Definition of Done as the acceptance-layer reference. Identify what the two internal auditors would expect to see.

## Deliverables

- A started AI system inventory containing at least the three named systems.
- Draft risk register entries for visual inspection and the customer service chatbot.
- At least two governed workflows: one for chatbot escalation and one for visual inspection disposition.
- A list of open items and the role responsible for finishing each, as recorded by participants.

All artifacts are drafts that the Client completes and owns. The program does not promise ISO/IEC 42001 certification, closure of the June 2026 audit finding or any audit outcome.

- **Customer data residency.** No customer data is used in any exercise. Chatbot work uses sanitized, invented transcripts written for the session, so no customer data enters the delivery environment.
- **Works council consultation.** Participants are governance practitioners, and no exercise involves production staff. The visual inspection workflow produced on Day 2 is a design draft only. Any implementation that touches production staff remains subject to works council consultation before it proceeds. The current consultation status of the visual inspection pilot is Unknown.
